# 64-structure E3-QE pilot on Lux: budget estimate

Estimate only; nothing here has been launched. QE per-label times come from the measured validation frames
(`e3-qe-lux-inputs/qe-timing.csv`, 1 Lux node, 8 ranks per frame). Geometry and MACE-training times are NOT measured
for E3-QE on Lux; the numbers below are assumptions, marked (assumed).

## What the loop actually spends per iteration

- `n_select: 16` and `n_iterations: 3` => **16 QE labels per iteration, 48 per material** (= `max_dft_calculations: 48`).
- Geometry stage (`benchmarks/e3/production.py`): the code requires **exactly 64 workers at exactly 4 workers per node**
  (`WORKERS_PER_ARRAY_ELEMENT = 4 x 16`, checks at production.py:2594 and :2600). On Lux that means **16 nodes with
  only 4 of the 8 GPUs used each**, unless those constants are made configurable (not done). It has a 15 min worker
  startup allowance, a 300 s hard limit per task and a 1800 s round deadline. These deadlines cover geometry only;
  QE labels are limited by `dft.qe.timeout_sec` alone (`qe_active_learning.py:136-152`, `:248-257`; `qe.py:129`).
- A label that exceeds `timeout_sec` fails, and `loop.fail_fast: true` stops the run. The retry-with-doubled-timeout
  rule (`execution/recovery.py:62`) is used only by `campaign/characterization.py`, not by this loop.
- 64 geometry tasks on 64 workers: one task per worker, so geometry is about one 300 s task wave plus startup.

Candidates generated by the sampler may differ in size from the validation frames (e.g. larger tiers), so treat these as
the best available proxy.

## Recommended Lux resources and Slurm request

- One allocation per material of **16 nodes** (geometry needs 16). Set `resources.pools.dft` to
  `{nodes: 16, devices: 8, max_concurrent: 16}`, one node per label, so 16 labels/iteration run in **one wave**
  (the current 8-node pool would need two waves). Training and MLIP stay on one GPU of the first node.
- Account `ven136`, partition `batch`, 1-day limit. Wall time per iteration = geometry (about 6 min + 15 min startup,
  assumed) + slowest label + MACE fine-tune (assumed 15 min) + overhead.

| Material | Iteration (approx.) | 3 iterations | Slurm request | Node-hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| phosphorene | 21 + 1 + 15 = ~40 min | ~2 h | 16 nodes, 3 h | ~48 |
| zn_formate_mof | 21 + 9 + 15 = ~45 min | ~2.3 h | 16 nodes, 3 h | ~48 |
| nb_ta_o | 21 + 13 + 15 = ~50 min | ~2.5 h | 16 nodes, 3.5 h | ~56 |
| bcc_refractory | 21 + 25 + 15 = ~60 min | ~3 h | 16 nodes, 4 h | ~64 |
| fcc_cantor | 21 + 155 + 15 = ~3.2 h | ~9.6 h | 16 nodes, 12 h | ~192 |
| lifepo4 | 21 + 145 + 15 = ~3 h | ~9 h | 16 nodes, 12 h | ~192 |

Pilot order: phosphorene first (cheapest, validated 64-structure behaviour), then nb_ta_o and zn_formate_mof.
FCC Cantor and LiFePO4 are about 4x more expensive and idle most of the 16 nodes while the slowest label finishes;
a smaller pool with multiple waves (e.g. 8 nodes, about 2x longer) could cost fewer node-hours but needs a longer wall time.

## Open points

- Make the 4-workers-per-node geometry constant configurable to use all 8 MI355X GPUs (8 nodes would then suffice).
- Per-material `timeout_sec` is set in `env-lux/<material>.sh` (14400 s for lifepo4 and fcc_cantor, 7200 s for bcc_refractory).
